# E2621 — a grammatical minimal-pair probe: can the instrument tell a trained decoder from an untrained one, and what does the trained one prefer?

**Status:** preregistered, not yet run. Committed before any checkpoint is scored; the Results section is added after.
**Type:** Measurement of **existing** checkpoints with a **new instrument**. Nothing is trained. It is the first of the capability instruments the project's own
benchmark specification ([`chakaso-capability-benchmarks.md`](../../docs/research/chakaso-capability-benchmarks.md)) lists as planned that can be run at this scale, and it is
chosen because its chance level is exactly one half and it needs no generation, prompt format or judge.

**Question.** The campaign's sharpest standing finding was that no instrument in the repository was known to detect a real change in what a model has learned: in E2612's data,
held-out loss and repetition moved in opposite directions. This record asks two narrower things. (1) **Validity:** does a likelihood-comparison probe over 800 self-authored grammatical
minimal pairs separate a trained decoder from an untrained one of the same architecture, and does it do so by more than a length bias? (2) **Description:** how do the five E2615/E2616 arms
and the E2617 control score on it? Only (1) is decision-bearing.

It measures one narrow thing — preference for a grammatical sentence over a minimally different ungrammatical one — on constructions common in 19th-century prose. It is not a measure of
understanding, knowledge, reasoning or usefulness (K-001).

## The instrument, fixed now

[`configs/probes/grammatical-minimal-pairs-v1.json`](../../configs/probes/grammatical-minimal-pairs-v1.json): 800 pairs, 100 in each of eight phenomena (subject–verb agreement; the same with an
intervening noun of the other number; determiner–noun number agreement; a modal taking the base form; determiner–adjective order; reflexive-pronoun agreement; past-participle form; auxiliary
agreement). Digest `d2ca34018df75aff2b5fe0c7327e73ece3b2a82b9d1e166d7581c75da4752632`, generated by `scripts/build_minimal_pairs.py`, every sentence written by this project. A pair is passed when
the model gives the acceptable sentence the higher summed log-probability, scoring each sentence as the continuation of a document separator (`sequence_log_probabilities`); a tie counts one half.
Accuracy carries a Wilson 95 % interval. The **length-only reading** is the accuracy of "prefer the shorter sentence" on the same pairs, independent of any model.

## Decision rules, declared before any model is scored

* **I-a — the instrument is valid at this scale.** For `E2617`: its Wilson interval **excludes 0.5**; and the **untrained control's interval includes 0.5**; and the `E2617` accuracy exceeds
  the length-only reading by more than the width of its own interval (that is, the length-only value lies below its interval's lower bound). Then the probe can see what a trained decoder
  has learned about these constructions, and its per-arm numbers below may be read as descriptions.
* **I-b — the trained model is at chance.** `E2617`'s interval includes 0.5. The probe cannot see learned grammar at 1.8 bits per character; it is recorded as unable to discriminate, and no per-arm reading is made.
* **I-c — the floor is not at chance.** The untrained control's interval excludes 0.5. The pair set has a bias that any model, trained or not, exploits (a token-frequency or length effect). The per-phenomenon control
  accuracies identify which phenomena carry it, and those are reported as **unusable** until fixed; the headline is not read.
* **I-d — anything else** (for example, the trained model clears chance but not the length-only reading). Reported with the numbers; the probe is not declared valid.

**Per-phenomenon usability (reported, applies under I-a).** A phenomenon is *usable* if the untrained control's interval for it includes 0.5 **and** the E2617 interval excludes it. A phenomenon that fails either
test is listed and excluded from any per-phenomenon reading.

**Descriptive predictions (stated so a surprise is recognisable; not decision-bearing).**

* **P1.** `E2617` scores above 0.75 overall. *Contradicted* by a lower value.
* **P2.** Agreement with an intervening attractor is the hardest phenomenon (lowest accuracy of the eight for `E2617`).
* **P3.** `E2615-s75` and `E2616` (prose-heavy) score at least as high as `E2615-s00` (no prose) overall; with one seed each, differences under the pair-count noise (about ±0.03 at 800 pairs) are not read.

**Contamination screen (reported, not decision-bearing).** The fraction of pairs whose acceptable sentence shares a 5-word n-gram with any document of the pinned corpus, with the flagged identifiers.
A flagged pair is not removed from the headline (the sentences are short and generic); the figure is there so a reader can see the size of the overlap, and a version of the accuracy over unflagged pairs is also reported.

**What may not change after a result is read:** the pair set, the scoring rule, the models, the outcomes and the predictions. A change to the pairs is a new version (v2) and a new record.

## Known limits, declared in advance

* One seed per model, so the arm comparison cannot separate composition from seed; the interval is for the *pairs* (sampling of sentences), not for training noise.
* The pairs are templated: 800 sentences are fewer degrees of freedom than their count suggests. The lexicon is small (16 nouns, 10 verbs).
* Nineteenth-century prose is the training register. A model that scores well has learned these constructions in that register; nothing is shown about modern English or any other language.
* Likelihood of a whole sentence rewards short sentences; the pairs differ by at most a few characters, and the length-only reading is reported for that reason.
* This is a probe of language-model *preference*, not of generation. A model can prefer the grammatical sentence and still generate nothing usable (K-001 stands).

## Cost

Seconds of CPU per checkpoint (1,600 short forward passes). No training.

## Reproduction

```bash
python scripts/evaluate_minimal_pairs.py --pairs configs/probes/grammatical-minimal-pairs-v1.json \
  --checkpoint E2617=data/experiments/E2617/seed-7/E2617-decoder.pt \
  --checkpoint E2615-s00=data/experiments/E2615-s00/seed-7/E2615-s00-decoder.pt \
  --checkpoint E2615-s25=data/experiments/E2615-s25/seed-7/E2615-s25-decoder.pt \
  --checkpoint E2615-s50=data/experiments/E2615-s50/seed-7/E2615-s50-decoder.pt \
  --checkpoint E2615-s75=data/experiments/E2615-s75/seed-7/E2615-s75-decoder.pt \
  --checkpoint E2616=data/experiments/E2616/seed-7/E2616-decoder.pt --control \
  --snapshot data/corpora/gutenberg-multiregister-v1/gutenberg-multiregister-v1-snapshot.json \
  --out experiments/E2621/results.json
```
